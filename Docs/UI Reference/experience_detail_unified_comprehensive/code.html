The TRIS Story

The Jingkieng Jri, or living root bridges, are not built; they are grown. This journey takes you deep into the valleys of Cherrapunji, where the Khasi people have guided the aerial roots of rubber fig trees across rivers for centuries.

It is a testament to sustainable living and generational patience. You won't just see a bridge; you will understand the profound philosophy of a community that builds for its grandchildren, respecting the forest's timeline over human convenience.

"We do not own these bridges. We are merely their caretakers, just as our grandfathers were, and just as our children will be. To cross it is to walk across time itself."

Bah Kupar

Village Elder & Guide, Nongriat

Cultural Significance

The Living Root Bridges are more than just a means of crossing rivers; they are living testaments to the indigenous ingenuity of the Khasi people. Grown over decades by manipulating the flexible roots of the Ficus elastica tree, these bridges embody a deep-rooted respect for nature and a sustainable approach to engineering.

This tradition is a profound example of long-term thinking, where each generation nurtures the growth for the benefit of the next, seamlessly blending human infrastructure with the natural environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is transportation to the starting point included? expand_more
Transportation to Tyrna village is not included in the standard price, but we can arrange an eco-friendly local cab pickup from Cherrapunji or Shillong at an additional cost.
Can children participate in this trek? expand_more
Due to the physical demands of the steep 3,000 steps, we recommend this trek for children over 12 years of age who are accustomed to active hiking.
What happens if there's an emergency on the trail? expand_more
All our local guides are trained in wilderness first aid and carry basic medical kits. We maintain constant communication with the village network for immediate assistance if required.
